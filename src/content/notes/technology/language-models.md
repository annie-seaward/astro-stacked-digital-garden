# Language Models

A language model is a probabilistic model of text. Large language models (LLMs) are neural networks trained on vast corpora of human-written text, learning to predict the next token given prior tokens.

## How they work

LLMs use the transformer architecture, introduced in "Attention Is All You Need" (Vaswani et al., 2017). Self-attention allows the model to weigh the relevance of each token in context.

Training involves predicting masked or future tokens across billions of examples. The result is a model that encodes a compressed representation of human knowledge and language patterns.

## Emergent capabilities

At sufficient scale, LLMs exhibit capabilities not explicitly trained for: multi-step reasoning, code generation, translation, and apparent common sense. Whether these reflect "understanding" or sophisticated pattern matching is debated.

## Philosophical implications

LLMs raise hard questions:

- Do they have beliefs? Intentions?
- Is their output a form of thought or mimicry?
- How does their deterministic computation relate to [[Free Will]]?

These questions connect to longstanding debates in [[Philosophy]] about mind, consciousness, and agency.

## The web connection

LLMs were largely trained on [[The Web|web]] content — the accumulated written output of humanity. In a sense, they are distillations of the web.
