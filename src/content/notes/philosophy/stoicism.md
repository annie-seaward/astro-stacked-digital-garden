# Stoicism

Stoicism is a school of Hellenistic philosophy founded in Athens by Zeno of Citium around 300 BC. It teaches that virtue is the only true good and that external events are indifferent.

## Core ideas

**The dichotomy of control**: Some things are up to us; others are not. Stoics focus only on what they can control — their judgments, desires, and actions.

**Virtue is sufficient for happiness**: External goods like wealth, health, and reputation are "preferred indifferents." They may be sought, but they don't determine flourishing.

**Live according to nature**: Human nature is rational. To live well is to live rationally, in accordance with logos.

## Key figures

- **Zeno of Citium** — founder
- **Epictetus** — formerly enslaved philosopher; wrote the *Enchiridion*
- **Marcus Aurelius** — Roman emperor; wrote *Meditations*
- **Seneca** — statesman and essayist

## Connections

Stoicism has influenced modern cognitive behavioral therapy. The idea that emotions arise from judgments — not events — maps directly onto CBT's cognitive restructuring.
